    The 10 Highest-Paid NBA Players in 2026-27

    • Dan
    • September 20th, 2026
    • No Comments
    • 2 Min Read

    Key Takeaways

    • Stephen Curry is the highest-paid player in 2026-27 at $62,587,158.
    • Ten players earn more than $56 million, all on maximum-level contracts.
    • Maximum salaries are a share of the cap: 25%, 30% or 35% by years of service.
    • The average salary across our 521 players is $11.24M and the median is $5.23M.
    • A single max salary can use more than a third of a team’s cap room.

    Ten players will earn more than $56 million in 2026–27, and every one of them is on a maximum-level contract tied to the salary cap. Here is the full top 10, taken from our NBA salary directory.

    The 10 Highest-Paid NBA Players

    # Player Team 2026–27 Salary
    1 Stephen Curry Golden State Warriors $62,587,158
    2 Nikola Jokić Denver Nuggets $59,033,114

    Stephen Curry tops the list at $62,587,158, the only salary above $60 million this season. Behind him, Nikola Jokić and Anthony Davis are less than $600,000 apart, and three players share an identical figure of $58,456,566. All three are on maximum contracts that started in 2025–26 at the 35% tier, two signed in 2023 and one in 2024, so they began at the same $54,126,450 and rise by the same $4,330,116 (8% of that first-year salary) each season.

    Why So Many Salaries Match

    Maximum salaries are not a single number. They are a share of the salary cap: 25% for players with up to six years of service, 30% between seven and nine years, and 35% from ten years on. Contracts that start in the same season at the same tier begin at the same salary, whenever they were signed, which is why the list is full of ties.

    Raises are capped too, so the gap between the top of the list and the rest grows slowly. The average salary across the 521 players in our directory is $11.24M and the median is $5.23M, so Curry earns more than five times the average and roughly 12 times the median.

    What a Max Contract Costs a Team

    A single maximum salary can take up more than a third of a team’s cap room. With the 2026–27 cap at $164,961,000, a $58 million player uses 35% of it before the rest of the roster is paid. That is why most teams carrying two max contracts end up above the luxury-tax line, which you can see on our team payroll table.
