    What Is Julius Randle’s Salary at Brooklyn in 2026-27?

    Randle earns $33.33m in 2026-27, up from $30.86m last season. He’s second on the Brooklyn payroll behind Michael Porter Jr.

    He’s been paid about $228.15m across thirteen seasons, which is more than anyone else on this roster. The salary accounts for 20.83% of Brooklyn’s $160.06m payroll, so he and Porter together take up close to half of it.

    What’s Next for Julius Randle Beyond 2026?

    Randle holds a player option on 2027-28 with a deadline of June 29, 2027. He'll be 32 then, which is late enough that one more multi-year contract is realistic but not certain. Declining it worked once, and the calculation will be the same.

    What Is Julius Randle’s Contract With the Nets?

    Randle signed a three-year contract on July 15, 2025 worth $100m, fully guaranteed, with a player option on the final season.

    It was signed through Bird rights, a few weeks after he declined a player option worth $30.94m on his previous deal. Turning down one guaranteed year to sign three is the trade most players in their thirties would take.

    Julius Randle’s Salary As a Percentage of Cap and Payroll

    Randle's $33.33m takes up 20.21% of the $164.96m salary cap and 20.83% of Brooklyn's $160.06m payroll. The cap is a benchmark, and clubs are allowed to spend past it under certain rules.

    What Is Julius Randle’s Net Worth?

    Estimated Net Worth
    $50M
    Published estimate

    Celebrity Net Worth puts Randle’s net worth at around $50m. He’s earned above $18m in every season since 2019-20, and above $28m in each of the last four.

    The distance from his $228.15m in career earnings is what tax, agent fees and league escrow take out. He’s been paid in California, Louisiana, New York and Minnesota, and New York in particular applies both state and city rates to a player’s income.

    Why Is Julius Randle Paid $33.33m?

    Randle is paid $33.33m because he scores and rebounds at a level very few forwards reach, and because he keeps proving it in the season before a contract.

    • He’s made All-NBA teams, which is the clearest evidence a club has that it isn’t overpaying.
    • He creates his own shot from the post and the perimeter, so an offense can be built around him rather than beside him.
    • His shooting has come and gone across seasons, and that variance is why the money sits below the maximum.
    • He was the seventh pick in 2014 and broke his leg in his first game, so his career started a full year late.
    • He declined $30.94m in June 2025 and signed for $100m three weeks later, which is the bet paying off.

    The player option on 2027-28 means he can do the same thing again at 33.
