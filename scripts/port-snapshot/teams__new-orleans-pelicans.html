    What Is the New Orleans Pelicans’ Payroll in 2026-27?

    New Orleans Pelicans salaries put $196.67 million on the books for 2026–27, which is $31.71 million above the $164.96 million salary cap. That figure includes $0.15 million in dead cap on top of a standard roster of $196.53 million.

    Zion Williamson tops the payroll at $42.17 million, ahead of Jordan Poole on $34.04 million and Dejounte Murray on $28.77 million, together about 53.4% of the roster total. Their tax salary is $3.75 million below the $200.43 million luxury-tax line and apron salary sits $5.92 million under the $209.02 million first apron.

    The New Orleans Pelicans standard roster accounts for $196.53M. Add $0.15M in dead cap to reach $196.67M before cap holds. Holds are separate accounting charges, not signed salary payments.

    What Does Going Over the Tax Line Mean?

    The luxury tax is an additional team expense. It rises in bands as tax salary moves above the threshold; teams with a recent history of paying tax can face higher repeater rates. The final bill depends on the season-end calculation.

    Why the Aprons Matter

    Aprons affect how a team can build its roster, including certain exceptions and trades. Being below an apron does not automatically create cap space, and a payroll figure alone does not establish whether a hard cap has been triggered.

    What Contracts Do the Pelicans Have on the Books?

    The contracts the Pelicans have on the books run mostly to 2027–28. Williamson’s designated rookie extension is worth $197.23 million and Murray’s veteran extension $114.24 million, both ending that season.

    Four deals expire after this one: Jordan Poole, AJ Johnson, Trendon Watford and Karlo Matković. Johnson joined the list on October 20, when New Orleans declined his 2027–28 club option. Herbert Jones and Saddiq Bey are carried furthest, to 2029–30, after Bey signed a three-year, $55.50 million extension in September. Trey Murphy III, Jeremiah Fears and Derik Queen run to 2028–29.
