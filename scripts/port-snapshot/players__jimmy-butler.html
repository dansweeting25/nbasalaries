    How Much Is Jimmy Butler III’s Salary in 2026-27?

    Jimmy Butler III is scheduled to earn $56,832,773 with the Golden State Warriors in 2026-27. That’s a 5% increase on his $54,126,450 salary in 2025-26 and the final year of his current deal. Butler is Golden State’s second-highest-paid player behind Stephen Curry, reflecting the price of an established two-way forward who can handle a major offensive role.

    His scheduled NBA base salary through 2026-27 totals $418,861,386. Butler’s 2026-27 salary accounts for 25.74% of the Warriors’ listed $220,838,432 active-roster base payroll. Chicago drafted him 30th overall in 2011, and his pay has risen from $1,020,960 as a rookie to more than $56.8 million in his current contract year.

    What’s Next for Jimmy Butler III Beyond 2026?

    Butler is scheduled to make $56,832,773 in 2026-27, his final contracted season with Golden State. He has no listed option, so he’s due to become an unrestricted free agent after that season at age 37. The Warriors can negotiate a new deal before free agency, while a trade remains another route if the club changes direction. His next contract will depend heavily on his health, availability, and 2026-27 production.

    What Is Jimmy Butler III’s Contract With the Warriors?

    Butler signed a two-year, $110,959,223 fully guaranteed veteran extension with Golden State on February 6, 2025. The deal was completed after his move from Miami and uses Bird rights. It began in 2025-26 and runs through 2026-27.

    The contract pays $54,126,450 in 2025-26 and $56,832,773 in 2026-27. Its scheduled base value and guarantee are the same, $110,959,223. Butler has no listed option in the agreement and is set to become an unrestricted free agent after the 2026-27 season.

    Why Is Jimmy Butler III the Warriors’ Second-Highest Earner?

    Butler’s salary reflects a proven ability to affect games in several demanding ways, particularly in a contender-focused role.

    • He developed from the 30th selection in the 2011 draft into a player repeatedly recognized at the All-NBA level.
    • His All-Defensive selections support the value of a forward capable of handling difficult perimeter assignments.
    • He can create offense with the ball, make plays for teammates, and work effectively in half-court possessions.
    • He can get to the free-throw line and create scoring opportunities when a half-court possession slows down.
    • His Eastern Conference Finals MVP recognition and Miami’s two Finals trips show that he has carried major responsibilities deep in the postseason.

    For Golden State, that combination makes him more than a complementary scorer beside Stephen Curry and Draymond Green.
